# Methodology / Delivery Approach

**Design Science Research (DSR)** is the primary methodology, because the project responds to an identified organisational problem by creating and evaluating an artefact rather than only producing a literature review (Hevner et al., 2004). Peffers et al. (2007)'s six-stage process structures delivery:

1. **Problem identification and motivation** — established via the Assessment 1 evidence review, NIST CSF comparison, likelihood×impact matrix, and Five Whys analysis.
2. **Objectives for a solution** — translated into requirements: six domains, 30 traceable questions, four maturity levels, evidence caps, critical gates, local-only processing, management-facing outputs.
3. **Design and development** — questionnaire/framework crosswalk, scoring rules, prototype, report format.
4. **Demonstration** — applied to two synthetic organisational scenarios: one with informal public-tool use and low governance maturity, one with approved enterprise tools but incomplete supplier/incident controls.
5. **Evaluation** — deterministic test cases (same inputs → same scores), domain calculation checks, critical-gate verification; findings recorded, prioritised, and traced to design changes.
6. **Communication** — final report, portfolio, and handover documentation.

**Why not Scrum-only:** Agile task management (GitHub backlog, weekly planning, short increments, regular review) supports execution discipline, but is not the primary methodology — shipping features isn't the success criterion here; whether the artefact is evidence-based and useful is.

**Why not a pure literature review:** insufficient on its own, since the project must build and evaluate a working capability, not just synthesise existing sources.

DSR provides the research logic; Agile practices provide transparent delivery discipline underneath it.
